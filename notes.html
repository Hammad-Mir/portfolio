System design

What annotation work changed in P&ID understanding

The original challenge looked like object detection. In practice, the larger obstacle was inconsistent symbol taxonomies across vendor legends. A detector trained on those labels learned the disagreements in the data.

Consolidating more than 1,100 legend entries into 12 standardized categories was a deliberate loss of surface detail. It made the system’s output more consistent, made classifier training tractable, and created a stronger basis for generalization to a new client’s drawings.

The practical lesson: when the task definition is unstable, improving the model is rarely the first lever to pull.

Retrieval

Designing retrieval around the document a technician needs

A hybrid retriever can return individually relevant chunks while still assembling an incoherent answer across several manuals. For maintenance questions, the most helpful context often belongs to one device and one document family.

Combining BM25 and semantic search with reciprocal-rank fusion improved recall. A content-aware reranking step then favored a coherent document source, while a locked context prevented later turns from drifting away from the identified device.

Research

Learning from image-level labels in pathology

Weak supervision is valuable because pixel-level labels are expensive, not because it removes the need for careful evaluation. Pseudo-masks should be treated as an intermediate hypothesis with known failure modes, especially in visually heterogeneous tissue.

Using vision-transformer attention to seed segmentation helped explore that hypothesis, but the important outcome was a better understanding of where weak labels preserve useful spatial information and where they do not.
